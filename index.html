5 incredible facts about dreams

From the science of memory to the possibility of controlling a dream, sleep is full of surprises.

FACT 01 / THE BRAIN 🧠

Why do we dream?

Dreams are among the brain's most intriguing mysteries. Researchers think they may help us process emotions, organize memories, and make sense of daily experiences. Vivid dreams often occur during REM (Rapid Eye Movement) sleep, when brain activity is high. Still, scientists do not fully understand why we dream or what every dream means.

Memory & emotions
FACT 02 / MEMORY ☁️

Why do dreams disappear?

You might wake up remembering a vivid dream, only to forget it minutes later. Dreams are not always stored as lasting memories, and the brain works differently during sleep than when we are awake. Remembering a dream soon after waking may help, while jumping into daily activities can make the details fade.

The fading story
FACT 03 / LUCID DREAMING ✨

Can you control a dream?

In a lucid dream, you realize that you are dreaming while the dream is still happening. Some people can influence the story—perhaps flying, exploring imaginary worlds, or changing what happens next. Researchers study lucid dreaming, and some techniques may help people experience it, but control is not guaranteed.

Awareness while dreaming
FACT 04 / NIGHTMARES 🌘

Why do nightmares happen?

Nightmares are frightening dreams that can leave us feeling scared or anxious. Stress, difficult experiences, some medications, and changes in sleep routines may play a role. A nightmare does not predict the future. Occasional nightmares are common, but frequent ones that disrupt sleep may be worth discussing with a health professional.

Fear & stress
FACT 05 / THE ANIMAL KINGDOM 🐾

Can animals dream too?

Many scientists think that some animals experience dream-like states. Dogs, cats, and other mammals have sleep patterns that include REM sleep. A sleeping dog moving its paws or making little sounds may be showing activity linked to dreaming. We cannot know exactly what animals dream about, but sleep may help them process memories and experiences.
